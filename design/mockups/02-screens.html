Answering your question: what does Flutter support natively?

I checked your installed SDK rather than trusting the docs. The colour half of Material 3 Expressive is native. The component half is not, and one detail matters: the expressive variant rotates the seed hue by 240 degrees, so your blue seed turns green. We are not going to use it. We keep tonalSpot's hue and take vibrant instead, which is the same idea (more chroma, more contrast) without throwing away your brand colour.

Native - one line, no packages

  • Colour scheme, dark mode, contrast tiers: ColorScheme.fromSeed(seedColor: <blue>, dynamicSchemeVariant: vibrant, contrastLevel: 0.0)
  • Type scale, shape, elevation, ripples, icon buttons, chips, sheets
  • Cupertino transitions, edge-swipe back, iOS sheets and scrolling
  • Material You wallpaper colour, straight from the phone, via dynamic_color

Ours to build - plain Flutter, no package

  • Keypad: press morph, haptics, long-press to clear
  • Converter: swap button, unit pills, rate chip, live re-conversion
  • Spring motion on the result line instead of an abrupt swap
  • History, and the grouped unit picker with search
  • Wavy progress and morphing loader: skip unless you want them

1 - 3: Calculator

Result first, at three times the old size. Keys are rounded squares in three tints so operators read at a glance, and the equals key carries the accent. The dead DEG and INV buttons are gone from the standard set - the scientific functions moved to a strip that only appears when you ask for it.

4 - 5: Converter

Input is neutral, the answer is tinted, so your eye lands on the result. The swap button sits between them instead of hiding in a menu, and the rate chip always shows its age.

6 - 7: Pickers

Search at the top, common choices first, then everything alphabetical. Your current pick is a tinted row, not a radio button buried on the right. First paint is instant even with 162 rows.

8 - 9: Hub and Settings

The current hub is a 3-column grid of look-alike tiles with no hint of what each one converts. Two columns with a one-line example reads instantly, and the units you actually use get promoted to the top.

What changes on iOS

Same layout, same colours, same keypad. Only behaviour and chrome adapt - which is what "works on both platforms" should mean.

Navigation

  • Swipe from the left edge to go back, with the interactive pop transition
  • Back chevron plus the previous screen's title, not a bare arrow
  • Sheets are draggable and dismiss by flinging down

Feel

  • Light haptic on every key press, medium on equals
  • Bouncing scroll physics and rubber-band ends
  • Home-indicator inset respected at the bottom of the keypad

Chrome

  • Large collapsing title on the hub, standard title elsewhere
  • No Android nav bar; a bottom tab bar with SF-style icons instead
  • Keyboard and text fields use the iOS convention
